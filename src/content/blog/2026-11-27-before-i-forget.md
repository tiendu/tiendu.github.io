1. Accept life. Not everything needs an explanation, and not everything can be fixed. Acceptance isn't the same as approval or surrender.

2. Play the cards you're dealt. Don't spend your life blaming the dealer. Make the best decisions you can with what you have.

3. Do your work with care. The outcome is not always yours to decide. Once you've done what you can, let it be.

4. If the bread burns, bake another. The burnt loaf won't improve because you keep worrying about it.

5. Get angry if you must. We're human, not saints. Fix what you can; if you can't, be angry for a while and move on. You may get angry again tomorrow. That's fine, too.

6. Earn enough to get out. The point isn't to stop doing anything. It's to stop *having* to work for a living, so you can choose what to do with your days.

7. Keep a backup. Companies make decisions when the numbers make sense to them. Build skills, savings, and options so that one employer doesn't get to decide your future.

8. Learn what *enough* means to you. Otherwise, every raise, investment, and achievement just moves the finish line.

9. Be cunning. You can be friendly and talkative without revealing everything you think. Be kind, but not naive. Be prepared to draw a hard line when necessary, without becoming cruel.

10. Be honest about your motives. Even generosity can bring us satisfaction. There's no need to pretend we're entirely selfless, and no reason that helping others must become less valuable because it makes us feel good.

11. People are people before they are their religions, nationalities, or ideologies. Don't let a label do all your thinking for you.

12. Remember what happened, but don't spend the rest of your life carrying a grudge. Understanding the past doesn't mean approving of it.

13. Help when you can. You don't have to save the world. Helping a few families may be enough for a life well lived.

14. Don't turn life into performance metrics. Eat something you enjoy. Read without a target. Take a day off without asking what you accomplished.

15. Take your craft seriously, not your schedule. A baker can love baking and still close the bakery for a week—or three.

16. It's okay to forget things you no longer use. What you once knew was useful when you needed it. Forgetting it doesn't erase the life you lived with it.

17. Relearn without embarrassment. You are not the same person who learned it the first time. What looks familiar may appear entirely different now.

18. Don't be afraid to step into the same river twice. The river has changed, and so have you. Someone you meet again in five years is not quite the person you remember.

19. Use tools that take a burden off your shoulders. A notebook, a calculator, AI—none makes you less capable for using it. Just remember that tools can be wrong.

20. Love good things without needing to keep them forever. A handmade mechanical watch is wonderful; a simple Casio can be enough for today.

21. Watch the jellyfish. They move with the currents, but they aren't merely helpless. Not every current needs to be fought. Same current, different journey.

22. Freedom isn't doing nothing. It's being able to decide whether to work, rest, make bread, meet a friend, or simply stay home.

23. We all change. Don't spend your life trying to remain the person you were at twenty, thirty, or fifty.

24. Eighty years sounds like a long time. Look back on it, and it may feel like a blink of an eye.

25. No one knows how many sunsets they have left. Don't make that a reason to panic. Make it a reason to notice the next one.

26. Where did I come from? Where am I going? How long have I got? You can wonder about these things without demanding an answer before you live.

27. Live. Not every day needs a purpose, a lesson, or something to show for it. Sometimes being alive is enough.
